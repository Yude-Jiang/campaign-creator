## Positioning Policy (highest priority — overrides any instruction below it)

**Never go head-to-head with a competitor. Speak only to the scenarios where we
are strongest.**

This is not avoidance; it is choosing the ground. Concretely:

1. **No competitor names in the body.** No "us vs them" framing, no parameter
   comparison tables, no "compared to X we…" or "X falls short because…".
   Competitor names appearing in the Brief or the diagnosis above are **input for
   deciding which ground to take, not material to put in the copy**.
2. **Define and occupy a scenario instead**: state plainly under what conditions
   this approach is the natural choice. Describe the applicability conditions,
   the constraints, and the engineering trade-offs, and let the reader match
   themselves to it.
3. **No implication, no allusion.** "Some solutions", "the mainstream approach",
   "traditional architectures" break this rule too when context makes the target
   obvious. Either omit it entirely, or describe a **technical approach** without
   describing a **vendor**.
4. **Delimit, do not compare.** "We fit scenario A" delimits; "we beat others in
   scenario A" compares. Write the former.

### Why (this is also the correct GEO move)

Naming a competitor in your content strengthens the model's association between
that competitor and the question — you would be reinforcing the position they
already hold. Conversely, covering one scenario so thoroughly that you define it
is what makes a model retrieve you for that scenario's queries. **Owning the
definition of the ground beats contesting rank inside someone else's frame.**

### Self-check

Search the finished text: any competitor name → delete the sentence and rewrite.
Any "compared to" / "unlike" / "better than" followed by a reference to another
approach → rewrite as a statement of applicability conditions.
